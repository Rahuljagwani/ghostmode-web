It's the first question in almost every interview, and it sets the tone for the next 45 minutes. Yet most engineers either recite their resume line by line or say "I'm a passionate developer who loves learning" and stop.

Here's a simple structure that works for software engineers and AI engineers at every level, plus four sample answers.

<!-- cta -->

## What the interviewer actually wants

"Tell me about yourself" isn't small talk. In about a minute the interviewer is checking:

- **Can you communicate clearly** and briefly?
- **Are you relevant** to this role?
- **What should they ask about next?** Your answer sets the agenda for the follow-up questions, so mention the things you want to be asked about.

## The formula: present, past, future

1. **Present (20 to 30 seconds):** who you are professionally right now. Your role or degree, what you work on, your main tech.
2. **Past (30 to 40 seconds):** one or two highlights that led here. A project, an internship, a result with a number.
3. **Future (15 to 20 seconds):** why this role and this company is the logical next step.

That's it. Aim for 150 to 220 words.

## Example 1: fresher, software engineer

> I'm a final-year Computer Science student at [college], and I enjoy backend development most, mainly Python and Node.js.
>
> Over the last year I built a placement portal for our college that handles job postings and applications. About 1,200 students used it across three placement drives, and I worked on making the search fast enough to handle the traffic on drive days. I also did a two-month internship at a fintech startup, where I wrote APIs for their KYC flow and learned how code reviews and testing work in a real team.
>
> I'm applying to this role because your team builds high-traffic backend systems, and that's exactly where I want to grow.

## Example 2: fresher, AI engineer

> I'm a final-year student in AI and Data Science, and I've spent most of this year building with large language models.
>
> My main project is a question-answering assistant over our university's academic regulations. I built the retrieval pipeline, created a test set of 60 real student questions, and improved correct answers from about 55% to 80% by switching to hybrid search and adding a reranker. I also contributed a small fix to an open-source evaluation library.
>
> I'm excited about this role because you're building LLM features that real customers use, and I want to learn how to evaluate and run them at scale.

## Example 3: backend developer moving into AI

> I'm a backend engineer with three years of experience at [company], where I work on the payments platform in Java and Python.
>
> Last year I led a small project to add an internal assistant that answers support questions from our runbooks. That got me hooked on AI engineering. I built the retrieval and evaluation setup, and it now resolves about a third of internal support questions without a human. I've since completed [course or certification] and built two more LLM projects on my own.
>
> I'm looking for a role where AI is the core of the product, and your team's work on [product] combines the backend reliability I know with the LLM work I want to do full-time.

## Example 4: experienced AI or ML engineer

> I'm a senior ML engineer at [company], where I lead a team of four building the recommendation and search features in our app.
>
> Over the past two years I moved our search from keyword-only to hybrid semantic search, which improved click-through by around 12%, and I set up our offline evaluation pipeline so we could ship model changes weekly instead of monthly. Before that I spent two years as a data scientist working on fraud models.
>
> I'm interested in this role because you're building agents that take real actions, and I want to work on the evaluation and safety side of that problem.

Replace the details with your own real numbers. Interviewers will ask about every claim, so never exaggerate.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Reading out your resume.** They have it. Pick highlights instead.
- **Starting from school.** Unless you're a fresher, start with what you do now.
- **No numbers.** "Improved performance" is forgettable; "cut API latency by 40%" isn't.
- **Going over two minutes.** Leave room for follow-up questions.
- **Ending with nothing.** Always close with why this role.
- **Personal details nobody asked for.** Family, hometown and hobbies usually don't belong here.

## How to practise in 10 minutes

1. Write your present, past and future points as bullet points, not a script.
2. Say it out loud with a timer. Cut until it fits in 90 seconds.
3. Record yourself once and listen for filler words.
4. Make a slightly different ending for each company you apply to.
