Recruiters often spend only seconds on a first look at a resume, and most of that time goes to your **bullet points**. Weak bullets list duties. Strong bullets show **what you achieved, how, and how much it mattered**.

This guide gives you a simple formula, 30 before-and-after examples, and a [free resume bullet point generator](/tools/resume-bullet-point-generator) that rewrites your bullets for you.

## The formula

![The resume bullet point formula: action verb, what, how, result, with a before and after example](/blog-images/resume-bullet-points-examples/formula.png "Lead with the verb, end with the number")

**Action verb + what you did + how + measurable result.**

- **Action verb:** built, reduced, automated, led, launched, redesigned.
- **What:** the system, feature, process or project.
- **How:** the tools or approach, briefly.
- **Result:** a number if you can: percent, time, money, users, scale.

**Before:** Responsible for the checkout API.
**After:** Rebuilt the checkout API with Redis caching and async payment calls, cutting p95 latency from 900 ms to 250 ms during peak sales.

## Action verbs to start with

- **Built:** built, developed, designed, launched, implemented, created.
- **Improved:** reduced, increased, optimised, accelerated, simplified, cut.
- **Led:** led, mentored, coordinated, owned, drove, organised.
- **Fixed:** resolved, debugged, migrated, automated, replaced, secured.

Avoid "responsible for", "worked on", "helped with" and "involved in". They describe a duty, not an achievement.

## Quick checks before you send

- **3 to 5 bullets per recent role**, most impressive first.
- **One or two lines each.**
- **A number in most bullets.**
- **Keywords from the job description**, so applicant tracking systems match you. Test your resume with our free [ATS resume checker](/tools/ats-resume-checker).
- **Consistent tense:** past tense for past roles, present tense for your current one.
